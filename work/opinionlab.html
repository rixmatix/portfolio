O-Lab

Helping brands hear their customers

For nearly two years I was at OpinionLab, where we helped companies gather feedback from their customers. In addition to collecting their Net Promoter Score, we could aggregate data to help brands make more timely and impactful decisions based on real customer pain points. We offered different forms of analysis of all that data: reports, consulting services, and a self-service dashboard application that I was in charge of.

Comment card

Where every data point started

Our company started with a feedback tab and a comment card. Living on our clients' websites, we were there for customers to leave their sentiment, give feedback and submit an NPS.

Our comment cards captured user data based on the Net Promoter Score as well as our own custom Likert scale. We tested multiple variations of scale, type and taxonomy, but ultimately found a 5-point scale worked best for our needs.

The main goal for the comment cards was accessibility. Per our contract agreements, the cards had to comply with the full Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG).

Data viz

A new application, one feature at a time

The new-generation application was still a brand new offering, slowly taking shape as we added more features. From a competitive standpoint we aimed to include the complexity of tools like Qualtrics and Looker, with the ease of use of Survey Monkey and Usabilla.

We had a web portal where clients accessed all of their data. The "new gen" application would temporarily live inside that interface until it could stand on its own.

O-Trend report

The 20-page report that set the roadmap

O-Trend was a 20-page report used by 75% of our clients to digest their data, and a lot of the features we prioritized came from this one report. It focused on specified sections, or custom reports, that tracked month-over-month and year-over-year trends to make user behaviour and engagement easy to spot. Our first goal was to improve filtering, so users could replicate the original report's views themselves.

I wanted an overview of everything O-Trend offered clients, to find ways to give them all the information they were used to while empowering them to do more.

Advanced filtering

Release the complexity gradually

Filtering was going to be a long, ongoing improvement. We planned to include all of our taxonomies, variables and custom IDs that clients could control. But we knew the upgrade would have to be released gradually, slowly increasing the complexity.

As with a lot of the projects I worked on, I started by learning from subject matter experts and looking at the data on current usage. Through surveys and internal critiques I gathered feedback on my decisions by showing work in progress.

Mapping complexity

Design the most complex state, then work backwards

The hardest part of managing all the changing components was having multiple versions of the product at once: the working web portal, a newer version we planned to release, and a future version for user testing. It was simpler to map out every component from its most complex state and work backwards, to make sure the patterns we were using would hold up.

Part of the challenge was our own Likert scale. There was a marketing need to prove its worth as a valuable and informative measure of how engaged our clients' users were with their brand.

Trending dashboards

Four views, one live filter bar

I decided to create four different dashboard views that together could hold the majority of the information in our O-Trend reports. Combined with a new live filtering system along the top, we could give users quick access to the metrics and views they used most.

I built four prototypes and circulated them with my team and our analytics department to get feedback on the views, and on the functionality needed to make them most useful.

Location dashboard

Stop catching up. Lead with something unique.

The truth was that our application was chasing a market with giants, and despite our efforts to come out with new features, we were really only catching up. Location services offered a trait that was unique in the market. Our new goal became releasing an industry-leading feature to bridge the gap between captured reviews and physical experiences.
